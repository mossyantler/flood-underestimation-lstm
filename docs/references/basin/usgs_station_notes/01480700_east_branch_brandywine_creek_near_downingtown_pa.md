Station/source summary:

- Station name: `01480700 East Branch Brandywine Creek near Downingtown, PA`.
- Location: Chester County, Pennsylvania, Hydrologic Unit `02040205`. The checked USGS station text places the gage on the right bank `20 ft` downstream from the Dowlin Forge Road bridge, `0.4 mi` downstream from Shamona Creek, `1.5 mi` downstream from Marsh Creek, `2.0 mi` upstream from Beaver Creek, and `2.2 mi` north of Downingtown.
- Drainage area: USGS lists `60.6 mi2`, close to the local CAMELSH/DRBC area of `155.21 km2`.
- Period of record: occasional low-flow measurements in water years 1948-1957, with continuous records from October 1965 to current year.
- Gage/datum: water-stage recorder, crest-stage gage, and water-quality monitor. Current station text lists gage datum `254.7 ft` above NAVD88 from a GNSS survey. The station text and 2024 Water-Year Summary note that the datum had previously been reported erroneously as `270 ft` above NGVD29 before February 19, 2025. Prior to July 30, 1966, a non-recording gage was used at the same site and datum.
- Regulation/diversion context: USGS station text states that flow has been regulated since November 1973 by Marsh Creek Reservoir, station `01480684`, `1.9 mi` upstream. The 2000 Water-Data Report repeats the same regulation remark. This is relevant because Marsh Creek enters upstream of this East Branch Brandywine gage.
- Rating/quality caveat: the 2024 Water-Year Summary says water-discharge records are rated fair except estimated discharges, which are poor. The 2000 Water-Data Report says records were good except estimated daily discharges, which were poor. The same 2000 report notes the 1999 instantaneous peak was from a rating curve extended above `5,000 ft3/s`.
- Cooperation: the station is operated with support from the Chester County Water Resources Authority, Pennsylvania Department of Environmental Protection Bureau of Safe Drinking Water, and USGS cooperative funding.

Use in basin diagnosis:

Treat this as a regulated East Branch Brandywine station with direct upstream Marsh Creek Reservoir influence, but not as a pure reservoir-outlet gauge. Event-scale interpretation should separate the regulated Marsh Creek tributary signal from the larger East Branch outlet response. If the target hydrograph peaks quickly with regional East/West Branch Brandywine gauges, reservoir regulation is a basin-context caveat rather than proof of target-level flood suppression. If dry-window plateaus, delayed pulses, or muted responses appear, Marsh Creek Reservoir and other mapped dams are credible managed-flow candidates, but no hourly reservoir operation record was reviewed here.
