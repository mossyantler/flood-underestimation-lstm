## Station facts

The official USGS station name is `East Branch Brandywine Creek below Downingtown, PA`. The site is on the left bank at the downstream side of Sugars Bridge on U.S. Highway 322, about `2,000 ft` upstream from Valley Creek, `1.5 mi` north of Marshallton, and `3.3 mi` southeast of Downingtown, Chester County, Pennsylvania. USGS lists a drainage area of `89.9 mi2`, consistent with the local CAMELSH/DRBC area of about `232.16 km2`.

Surface-water records begin in February 1972 and continue to the present on the checked USGS pages. The gage is described as a water-stage recorder and crest-stage gage; the legacy current-conditions page also lists a water-quality monitor and Pluvio precipitation gage. The datum is `195 ft` above NGVD29 from topographic map. USGS notes nonrecording gage periods at the same site and datum during parts of 1972.

## Regulation and water-use context

The key station-context fact is direct regulation. The USGS current-conditions page, the Water-Year Summary page, and the 2003 Water-Data Report all state that flow has been regulated since November 1973 by Marsh Creek Reservoir, station `01480684`, about `7.5 mi` upstream.

The USGS 2005 water-budget report adds broader context for the East Branch Brandywine Creek watershed: the watershed has reservoir storage and fractured-rock hydrogeology, and the report estimates that sewage-treatment-plant and industrial discharges plus Marsh Creek Reservoir regulation increased apparent base flow over the 1977-2001 water-budget period. This supports treating dry-window extra flow or elevated baseflow as plausible context, but it does not provide event-level reservoir release records.

The USGS 2023 Ida flood report lists the September 1, 2021 peak at this station as `21,000 ft3/s` with an estimated annual exceedance probability around `0.2%`. That source supports treating the 2021 event as a real regional extreme flood, not merely a local release-like artifact.

## Data-quality notes

The current station page and WDR PDF identify estimated discharges as lower-quality periods in some water years. The checked annual summary text lists water-year record quality as fair or good in several recent years, with estimated discharge commonly rated poor. No hourly reservoir operation, storage, gate, or diversion-operation records were reviewed for this note.
