Station/source summary:

- Station name: `01480300 West Branch Brandywine Creek near Honey Brook, PA`.
- Location: Chester County, Pennsylvania, Hydrologic Unit `02040205`, on the right bank upstream from the SR 4007 bridge at Birdell, `0.4 mi` downstream from Two Log Run, and `3.0 mi` southeast of Honey Brook.
- Drainage area: USGS lists `18.7 mi2`, close to the local CAMELSH/DRBC area of `47.66 km2`.
- Period of record: surface-water records begin in June 1960 and continue to current year.
- Gage/datum: water-stage recorder and crest-stage gage. Current station text also lists a water-quality monitor. Datum is `591.20 ft` above NGVD29. The Water-Year Summary and 2002 Water-Data Report state that before July 1990 the water-stage recorder was at a site `130 ft` downstream on the right bank at the same datum.
- Cooperation/funding: the station is established and maintained by USGS, with funding listed from Pennsylvania Department of Environmental Protection, Chester County Water Resources Authority, and USGS cooperative matching funds.
- Regulation/diversion remarks: the key official remark is low-flow regulation by pumpage from a treatment plant. The 2002 Water-Data Report identifies this as pumpage from the Northwestern Chester County Wastewater Treatment plant. The checked USGS sources do not identify a flood-control reservoir or large high-flow regulation structure for this site.
- Rating/quality caveat: the 2002 Water-Data Report says records were fair except discharges above `1,000 ft3/s` and estimated daily discharges, which were poor. It also notes that the January 19, 1996 maximum peak flow of `3,800 ft3/s` came from a rating curve extended above `1,000 ft3/s` using runoff comparison with nearby stations. The Water-Year Summary says water years 2014-2016 are good except discharges less than `10 ft3/s` and estimated daily discharges, and that water years 2018-2020 and 2022 are fair except estimated discharge.

Use in basin diagnosis:

Treat this as an upper West Branch Brandywine Creek station with explicit low-flow pumpage context, but not as a documented flood-control or reservoir-attenuated station. Local metadata point the same way: the StreamStats cache reports `isRegulated = false`, GAGES-II dam attributes show no mapped dams or major dams in the basin, and local storage proxies are small.

The treatment-plant pumpage remark is relevant for low-flow plateaus, dry-window small pulses, or baseflow offsets. It should not be used alone to explain event-scale flood peak suppression. For flood events, use rain timing, observed peak timing, downstream West Branch Brandywine comparisons, and the high-flow rating caveat before assigning any managed-flow cause.
