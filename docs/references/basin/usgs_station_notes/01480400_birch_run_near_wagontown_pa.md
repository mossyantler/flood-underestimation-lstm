## Station facts

USGS identifies the station as `01480400 Birch Run near Wagontown, PA`. The site is in Chester County, Pennsylvania, Hydrologic Unit `02040205`, on the right bank `15 ft` upstream from SR 4005, `0.2 mi` upstream from the mouth, `0.6 mi` downstream from Chambers Lake, and `1.1 mi` northwest of Wagontown. The official drainage area is `4.55 mi2`, close to the local CAMELSH/DRBC area of `12.0384 km2`.

The period of record begins in February 1995. The current station text describes a water-stage recorder, water-quality monitor, crest-stage gage, and concrete control, with gage elevation `505.81 ft` above NAVD88. The current monitoring page lists the site as operated in cooperation with the Chester County Water Resources Authority.

The critical hydrologic context is regulation. The current USGS monitoring page states that flow is regulated by Chambers Lake, station `01480399`, `0.6 mi` upstream. The 2003 Water-Data Report repeats the same regulation remark and rates the water-discharge records as fair, with no estimated daily discharges for that water year. A legacy 2000 report also used the name Chambers Lake Reservoir for the upstream regulator.

The upstream Chambers Lake page identifies station `01480399 Chambers Lake near Wagontown, PA` at Hibernia Dam on Birch Run, `0.6 mi` upstream from station `01480400`, with drainage area `4.50 mi2`. The indexed Water-Year Summary text describes the reservoir as formed by an earthfill dam with principal spillway at elevation `587.5 ft` and capacity `2,000 acre-ft`.

The station page notes that no NWS flood stage has been determined for this station. The legacy page also lists a USGS data-transmitter operational limit of `11.6 ft` and an approximate `9.5 ft` Lion's Head Road low point at the gage. No reviewed source provides an hourly Chambers Lake operation or release record for the event windows diagnosed here.

## Diagnosis implication

For event-scale hydrograph diagnosis, this station should be treated as a small regulated headwater outlet immediately below Chambers Lake/Hibernia Dam. Strong simulated quick runoff after extreme rain is not, by itself, evidence that observed flow is wrong; the station context supports reservoir attenuation and managed storage/release as a primary explanation for suppressed observed peaks. However, without reservoir operation records, event-specific claims should be limited to regulated attenuation/recession behavior rather than exact release decisions.
