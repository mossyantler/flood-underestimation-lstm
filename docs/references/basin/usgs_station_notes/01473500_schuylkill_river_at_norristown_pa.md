# 01473500 - Schuylkill River at Norristown, PA

## Sources checked

- USGS monitoring location page: https://waterdata.usgs.gov/monitoring-location/USGS-01473500/
- USGS Water-Data Report 2013 PDF: https://wdr.water.usgs.gov/wy2013/pdfs/01473500.2013.pdf

## Station facts

The USGS monitoring page identifies the station as `Schuylkill River at Norristown, PA - USGS-01473500`. It also lists cooperative operation with the City of Philadelphia Water Department, the US Army Corps of Engineers Philadelphia District, and USGS Federal Priority Streamgages.

The 2013 Water-Data Report identifies the site as `01473500 SCHUYLKILL RIVER AT NORRISTOWN, PA` in the Lower Delaware Basin, Schuylkill Subbasin. The location is on the left bank at the Haws Avenue bridge leading to Barbadoes Island, `0.2 mi` upstream from Stony Creek and `0.6 mi` upstream from Norristown Dam. The listed drainage area is `1,760 mi2`, consistent with the local CAMELSH/DRBC area of about `4560.57 km2`.

The surface-water period of record in the 2013 report is August 2001 to current year, with an older October 1927 to May 1933 record at a site `0.6 mi` downstream and at a different datum. Annual maximums from October 1983 to September 1993 came from a crest-stage gage `0.7 mi` downstream at a different datum. The gage is a water-stage recorder with a datum of `51 ft` above NGVD29 from topographic map, with satellite and landline telemetry.

The 2013 report remarks for discharge records state that records were good and that there were no estimated daily discharges for that water year. I did not find a direct station-level remark in the checked USGS sources saying that discharge at this gage is regulated by a named reservoir or diversion. This does not rule out upstream managed-flow influence across the large Schuylkill basin; it only means the checked station text does not provide direct event-operation evidence.

## Use in basin diagnosis

For event diagnosis, treat this as a large Schuylkill mainstem gage with short modern discharge coverage in the local CAMELSH record, several older stress events lacking rated streamflow coverage, and substantial upstream basin complexity. Local hydromodification proxies are strong, but the official source check supports conservative language: use `mixed large-mainstem / hydromodified context` rather than claiming a specific reservoir release unless separate operation records are reviewed.
