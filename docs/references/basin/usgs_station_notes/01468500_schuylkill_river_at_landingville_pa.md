# 01468500 Schuylkill River at Landingville, PA

Source check completed for the event-suppression diagnosis wave.

## Official station sources

- USGS Water Data for the Nation monitoring location: https://waterdata.usgs.gov/monitoring-location/USGS-01468500/
- USGS NWIS site inventory: https://waterdata.usgs.gov/nwis/inventory/?site_no=01468500
- USGS Water-Data Report 2000 PDF: https://pubs.usgs.gov/wdr/2000/dela-00/wdr_del-2000/pdfs/01468500.pdf

## Station facts

- Station name: Schuylkill River at Landingville, PA.
- USGS drainage area: 133 mi2, matching the local CAMELSH scale of about 344.21 km2.
- Location note from the water-data report: left bank at Landingville, 0.1 mi upstream from Mahannon Creek and 5.0 mi downstream from West Branch Schuylkill River.
- Period of record in the 2000 report: August 1947 to April 1953, October 1963 to September 1965, and August 1973 onward. The NWIS inventory lists daily discharge from 1947-10-01 through the current published record.
- Gage/datum: the NWIS inventory reports datum of gage as 469.97 ft NAVD88. The 2000 report used 470.64 ft above sea level/NGVD29 convention and notes a pre-1947 nonrecording gage 10 ft downstream at the same datum.

## Remarks and caveats

- The 2000 water-data report says records were good except estimated daily discharges, which were fair, and notes satellite/landline telemetry.
- A current USGS daily-data page snippet for this station reports that some low-flow regulation may occur by mine pumpage above the gage.
- The 2000 report does not list major reservoir regulation for this station. It does note the June 1972 flood outside the period of record and that the historical instantaneous peak was from a rating curve extended above 5,000 ft3/s.

## Interpretation for basin diagnosis

The source check supports a mostly natural-to-mixed upper Schuylkill mainstem interpretation rather than a station with explicit USGS reservoir regulation. Mine pumpage, mining land cover, dams in the GAGES-II hydromod attributes, and water-use proxies should be treated as secondary context, especially for low-flow or dry-window behavior. Event-scale flood suppression or extra-flow claims should remain conservative unless supported by the hydrograph and nearby-gauge comparison.
