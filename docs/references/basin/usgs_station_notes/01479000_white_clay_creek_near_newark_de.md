Station/source summary:

- Station name: `01479000 White Clay Creek near Newark, DE`.
- Location: New Castle County, Delaware, Hydrologic Unit `02040205`. The USGS station text places the gage on the left bank, `0.4 mi` downstream from the Baltimore and Ohio Railroad bridge, `1.1 mi` downstream from Pike Creek, `3.8 mi` east of Newark, and `5.0 mi` upstream from the mouth.
- Drainage area: USGS lists `89.1 mi2`, matching the local CAMELSH/DRBC area of about `230.78 km2`.
- Period of record: USGS station text lists October 1931 to September 1936, June 1943 to September 1957, and October 1959 to current year, with monthly discharge only for some periods. The inventory lists daily discharge beginning `1931-10-01`, peak streamflow records through water year 2024, and Water-Year Summary availability for water years `2006-2024`.
- Gage/datum: water-stage recorder and crest-stage gage. The monitoring page lists the current gage datum as `7.86 ft` above NAVD88 and `9.00 ft` above NGVD29. The Water-Data Report 2013 notes that earlier records through April 7, 1976 were collected at a site `0.5 mi` upstream at a gage datum `2.6 ft` higher.
- Cooperation/funding: the monitoring page lists operation in cooperation with the Delaware Geological Survey, USGS Cooperative Matching Funds, and USGS Observing Systems Division.
- Regulation/diversion remarks: the official station text says flow is affected by the City of Newark municipal water treatment plant upstream from the station and by periodic withdrawals to maintain storage in the City of Newark's pumped storage reservoir. This is direct station-level evidence of managed water-use influence, but the text does not describe a flood-control reservoir immediately regulating the outlet. The local StreamStats cache reports `isRegulated = false`.
- Rating/quality caveat: the Water-Data Report 2013 describes records as good except estimated daily discharges affected by backwater and ice, which are poor. It also notes an outside-period maximum stage in July 1937 that was probably affected by railroad-bridge backwater at the previous site.

Use in basin diagnosis:

Treat this as a larger White Clay Creek station with a long discharge record, downstream routing and tributary context, and explicit municipal water-use influence. The official station note supports caution for low-flow, recession, and dry-window interpretation because withdrawals to a pumped storage reservoir can alter natural flow. It does not by itself support diagnosing the extreme-rain flood peaks as reservoir-regulated suppression or managed release. Event-scale claims should therefore require hydrograph evidence and nearby White Clay Creek comparisons.
