The 2001 report remarks are important for diagnosis. Records are described as good below `100 ft3/s` and above `4,000 ft3/s`, fair between `100` and `4,000 ft3/s`, and poor for estimated daily discharges affected by backwater and ice. The report also notes occasional low-flow regulation from an unknown upstream source. For water year 2001, the report lists peak discharges above the `1,000 ft3/s` base discharge on `2000-12-17`, `2001-03-30`, and `2001-06-16`; the annual maximum was `1,340 ft3/s` on `2001-06-16`.

## Diagnostic implications

This station should be treated as a very small, heavily urbanized Wilmington-area basin with long discharge record support. The official source check does not identify a named upstream reservoir or a major regulation project. It does, however, explicitly notes occasional low-flow regulation from an unknown upstream source, so dry-window low-flow pulses or plateaus should not be interpreted as natural runoff without caution.

For extreme-rain hydrograph diagnosis, the stronger station-level context is urban flash response plus possible minor low-flow management, not a known reservoir-regulated outlet. Local hydromod metadata should therefore be used as secondary context unless event hydrographs and nearby comparisons independently show a storage/release pattern.
